    What to do

    Pick the place where you found the plant. Safety notes come first.

    Removing a plant from your own plot

    1. Identify it

      Confirm the species with the identifier or the catalog. Control methods differ a lot between species.

    2. Protect yourself

      Wear long sleeves, gloves and eye protection. For hogweed use a full protective suit, cover your face and work on a cloudy day or in the evening.

    3. Remove it

      Choose a method from the plant’s page: digging out, mowing or cutting, a competing sown cover, or a herbicide used strictly as the label says.

    4. Dispose of it

      Do not compost seed heads, roots or rhizomes. Bag them or dry them completely, and check local waste rules before burning anything.

    5. Check back

      Look over the area every few weeks in the growing season. Seed banks in the soil can keep producing seedlings for several years.

    Mechanical

    Dig out roots with a spade or fork, pull seedlings by hand, mow before flowering.

    Chemical

    Use only products approved for your use and follow the label and local regulations.

    Agronomic

    Sow a dense grass mix or plant competitive native species to keep bare ground covered.

    Finding a plant in an apartment courtyard

    Housing office

    Start with your housing maintenance office or building management company. They look after the land around the building.

    City greening service

    The organisation that maintains city green spaces. Look for a request form on the city’s official website.

    Environmental inspectorate

    The regulator for environmental protection. Contact your district office if the first two do not respond.

    What to put in your report

    1. Clear photos from several angles, with something in frame for scale.
    2. The location: address and nearby landmarks.
    3. Roughly how many plants there are and how big the patch is.
    4. The species name if you identified it, and the date you saw it.
    5. How they can reach you.

    Finding a plant in a park or nature reserve

    Do not remove it yourself

    Digging in protected areas can damage native plants and is often not allowed. Report the find instead.

    1. Photograph the plant and include landmarks so someone can find the spot.
    2. Identify the species with the identifier.
    3. Tell the park or reserve administration. Their contact details are on the city’s official website.
    4. Send the coordinates. Most phones can show them in the map app.

    What to plant instead

    Native or well-behaved alternatives for the most troublesome species.

    Where to find native plants

    • Nurseries that grow local-provenance plants.
    • Garden centres with a native-species section.
    • Plant swaps with other gardeners, as long as nothing on the swap list is invasive.
